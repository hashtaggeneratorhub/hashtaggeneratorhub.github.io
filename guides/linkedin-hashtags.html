LinkedIn Hashtags: A Professional's Guide

LinkedIn is a professional network, and its hashtag culture reflects that. Tags here are quieter, fewer, and more focused on industries, skills, and ideas than on trends or entertainment. Used well, they put your posts in front of hiring managers, clients, and peers who follow those topics.

This guide explains how LinkedIn hashtags work, how many to use, which kinds professionals follow, and how to use them without looking like you wandered in from Instagram.

How hashtags work on LinkedIn

On LinkedIn, you can follow a hashtag, and posts using it appear in your feed. That makes tags a genuine distribution channel: a post tagged #projectmanagement can reach people who follow that topic but do not follow you. LinkedIn also suggests hashtags when you write a post, based on the text.

Unlike Instagram, LinkedIn shows the follower count for a tag, which tells you its size. A tag with 5 million followers is broad; one with 50,000 followers is a focused professional community. Both have value, but the smaller one is where your post is more likely to be noticed.

How many hashtags to use on LinkedIn

Three to five hashtags per post is the standard on LinkedIn. More than that looks unprofessional and distracts from the message. Put the tags at the end of the post or woven into the text, not in a separate block, because LinkedIn readers expect a clean, article-like format.

Choose tags that match the post's professional topic: the industry, the skill, and the idea. A post about leading a remote team might use #leadership, #remotework, and #management. Avoid generic tags like #love or #instagood, which signal that the poster does not understand the platform.

Which hashtags professionals follow

Professionals follow tags for their industry, their role, and their interests: #marketing, #datascience, #healthcare, #leadership, #careeradvice, and similar. They also follow tags for events and movements in their field. The best tags for you are the ones your target audience follows, not the ones with the biggest numbers.

To find them, look at the posts of people you admire in your field and note the tags they repeat. Check the follower count on each tag page, and read the recent posts to confirm the tag is used by professionals, not by spammers selling unrelated services.

Hashtags for job seekers and businesses

Job seekers can use tags to join conversations in their target industry and to be found by recruiters who follow those tags. A post about a project, tagged with the relevant skill and industry tags, can reach hiring managers directly. Keep the tone professional and the tags relevant to the work, not the job search itself.

Businesses can use tags to reach buyers and partners, and to build authority by posting useful content under industry tags consistently. A branded tag can collect employee and customer posts, but it works on LinkedIn only if the company actively uses it and features the people who post with it.

Key takeaways

  • Use 3 to 5 professional hashtags per LinkedIn post, at the end or woven into the text.
  • Follow the tags your target audience follows, and check their follower counts.
  • Keep tags focused on industry, skill, and ideas — not generic or lifestyle tags.
  • Post useful content under industry tags consistently to build authority.

Frequently asked questions

How many hashtags should I use on LinkedIn?

Three to five per post. More than that looks unprofessional and distracts from the message.

Can people follow hashtags on LinkedIn?

Yes. Followed hashtags appear in your feed, which makes them a real distribution channel for reaching people who do not follow you.

Should job seekers use hashtags on LinkedIn?

Yes, sparingly. Tag the skill and industry in your posts so recruiters who follow those topics can find your work.
