Seasonal and Trending Hashtags: How to Ride the Moment

Some hashtags appear every year, like holiday tags, and some appear for a day, like a viral challenge or a breaking news tag. Both can bring a surge of viewers, but only if your post genuinely fits the moment. A forced trend post is easy to spot, and audiences punish it with quick scrolls.

This guide explains how to find seasonal and trending hashtags, how to judge whether they fit your brand, and how to join a trend in a way that feels natural.

Finding seasonal hashtags early

Seasonal hashtags follow the calendar: holidays, sports seasons, school terms, and shopping events. Plan them a month ahead. Search last year's version of the tag to see what people posted and which posts performed well, then plan your own version with a fresh angle.

Keep a simple calendar with the dates that matter to your audience and the tags for each one. A food creator's calendar looks different from a fitness creator's, so build yours around your own audience's year, not a generic list.

Finding trending hashtags right now

Check the trending or Discover page on TikTok, X, and Instagram each morning if trends matter to your niche. Look at the top posts before you join: what kind of content is succeeding, and is the trend a joke, a challenge, a song, or a news event? The format matters as much as the tag.

Google Trends is useful for checking whether a trend is rising or already falling. A trend that peaked yesterday has far less opportunity than one that is still climbing. Speed matters, but only when the fit is real.

Judging whether a trend fits your brand

Ask three questions before joining a trend. Does it match what I post about? Would my audience find this interesting or confusing? Is the tone right: funny, serious, or celebratory? If the answer to any of them is no, skip it. Missing a trend costs nothing; joining the wrong one can cost trust.

Be especially careful with trends tied to tragedies, disasters, or sensitive news. Using a serious tag to sell a product is one of the fastest ways to damage a brand, and screenshots last far longer than the trend.

Joining a trend without looking forced

The best trend posts add something original: your expertise, your humour, or your own twist on the format. Copying the top video frame by frame adds nothing, and viewers have already seen it. Ask what your version can say that nobody else's can.

Post early, while the trend is still climbing, and keep the rest of your content normal. One trend post among regular posts looks like a creator having fun; ten trend posts in a row looks like someone chasing views.

Key takeaways

  • Plan seasonal hashtags a month ahead using last year's tag pages.
  • Check Google Trends to see whether a trend is rising or already falling.
  • Skip any trend that fails the fit test: topic, audience, and tone.
  • Add your own angle to a trend instead of copying the top post.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find trending hashtags?

Check the Discover or trending pages on TikTok, X, and Instagram, and use Google Trends to see whether a topic is rising or falling.

Should brands join every trend?

No. Join only trends that match your topic, audience, and tone. Missing a trend costs nothing; joining the wrong one can damage trust.

When is the best time to post a trend?

Early, while the trend is still climbing. A trend on day one has far less competition than the same trend on day five.
