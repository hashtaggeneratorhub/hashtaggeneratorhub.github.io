How to Use Hashtags: A Complete Beginner's Guide

A hashtag is a word or phrase with a hash sign in front of it, like #travel or #homemadefood. When you add one to a post, the platform files that post under the hashtag, so anyone searching for or following that tag can find it. That simple mechanism is why hashtags remain one of the cheapest ways to reach people who do not follow you yet.

Hashtags work on Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, and Facebook, but the rules are different on each platform. A strategy that works on Instagram can look spammy on X, and a tag that trends on TikTok may mean nothing on LinkedIn. This guide explains the basics that apply everywhere, and the differences that matter.

What a hashtag actually does

When you publish a post with a hashtag, the platform adds it to a public feed for that tag. People who search the tag, follow it, or tap it on someone else's post can discover your content. In that sense a hashtag is a label and a distribution channel at the same time: it describes the post and it puts the post in front of an interested audience.

Hashtags also help platforms understand what your content is about. If you consistently post baking videos with tags like #sourdough and #breadbaking, the platform learns your topic and is more likely to show your work to people who watch baking content. That is why relevance matters more than popularity: a tag that matches your content teaches the algorithm who your audience is.

Where to put your hashtags

On Instagram, hashtags can go in the caption or in the first comment. Both work, and the first comment keeps the caption clean. On TikTok and YouTube, tags go in the caption or description. On X and LinkedIn, tags belong inside the post text itself, where they read naturally as part of a sentence.

Avoid hiding tags in a separate block of unrelated words at the end of a thoughtful post on LinkedIn or X, because readers there expect tags to be part of the conversation. On Instagram and TikTok, a block of tags at the end is normal and accepted. The rule is simple: match the habits of the platform you are posting on.

How many hashtags to use

Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags per post, but using all 30 is rarely wise. Most creators see the best results with 5 to 15 relevant tags. TikTok works best with 3 to 6. X works best with 1 or 2. LinkedIn works best with 3 to 5. YouTube allows up to 15 in a description, though only the first 3 show above the title.

Start with fewer tags and add more only if your reach is low. If a post performs well with 5 tags, there is no reason to add 25 more. Count what matters: are the tags bringing viewers from search and tag pages, or are they just decoration? Your analytics will tell you.

Choosing tags that fit your post

A good hashtag describes the actual content of the post. A photo of a sourdough loaf deserves #sourdough, #breadbaking, and #homebaker, not #foodporn alone, because the specific tags reach people who care about baking. Broad tags have huge audiences, but your post disappears from them in seconds. Specific tags have smaller audiences, but your post stays visible longer.

Build a small library of tags for each topic you post about, and rotate them. Using the exact same set on every post can look automated, and platforms may treat repeated identical tag blocks as spam. Keep 3 or 4 core tags per topic and swap the rest based on the specific post.

Key takeaways

  • A hashtag is a label that files your post under a topic other people can find.
  • Match tag count and placement to the platform: Instagram 5-15, TikTok 3-6, X 1-2, LinkedIn 3-5.
  • Relevant, specific tags beat popular, generic ones for most creators.
  • Rotate your tag sets instead of pasting the identical block on every post.

Frequently asked questions

Do hashtags still work in 2026?

Yes. Platforms still use hashtags for search, discovery, and topic grouping, though algorithms now also read captions, audio, and images. Hashtags are one signal among several, not the whole strategy.

Should I put hashtags in the caption or the first comment on Instagram?

Both work the same for discovery. The caption is simpler and safer, because a first comment can be missed if comments load slowly. Use the caption unless you prefer a cleaner look.

Can hashtags get my account banned?

Hashtags alone rarely cause a ban, but using banned tags, repeating identical tag blocks, or tagging unrelated popular topics can limit a post's reach or trigger spam filters.
