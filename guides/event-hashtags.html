Event Hashtags: Promote Any Event on Social Media

An event hashtag is a meeting point. It collects every photo, video, and comment about your event in one searchable place, lets attendees find each other, and gives you content to share long after the event ends. Without one, the conversation scatters across dozens of untagged posts.

This guide covers the full timeline: choosing the tag, launching it before the event, using it during the event, and keeping the conversation going afterwards.

Choosing the event hashtag

Pick a tag that is short, unique, and easy to type on a phone. Include the event name or a short version of it, plus the year if the event repeats, so this year's posts do not mix with last year's. Search the tag on every platform first: if someone else uses it, choose another.

Test the tag with a few people who have not seen it before. Can they spell it after hearing it once? Does it read clearly without spaces? Print it large enough to read from the back of a room, because it will go on screens, banners, and badges.

Before the event: build anticipation

Announce the hashtag when you announce the event, not the day before. Use it on every promotional post, in the event description, and in emails to attendees and speakers. Ask speakers to include it in their slides and their own posts, because their audiences are your future attendees.

Run a small pre-event activity around the tag: a countdown, a behind-the-scenes series, or a question thread. The goal is to have real posts under the tag before the doors open, so the first attendees see an active conversation rather than an empty page.

During the event: make it visible and rewarding

Put the hashtag on every screen, on the stage backdrop, on table cards, and in the event app. Announce it from the stage at the start of each session. People cannot use a tag they cannot see, and a single mention is easy to miss.

Reward participation in real time. Show a live feed of tagged posts on screens, repost the best attendee photos, and run a small prize for the best post of the day. A live display creates a loop: people see their post on the big screen, and others post to join in.

After the event: keep the conversation alive

The event ends, but the hashtag should keep working. Share a highlights reel built from attendee posts, with credit, and thank the people who posted. Post the photos, the slides, and the key quotes under the tag over the following week, so late visitors still find value.

Measure the tag: how many posts used it, how many people saw them, and which posts performed best. Share the numbers with sponsors, because tagged posts are proof of reach. Then save the tag for next year, with the year updated, so the archive grows.

Key takeaways

  • Choose a short, unique tag and search it on every platform before committing.
  • Announce the tag with the event itself, not the day before.
  • Display the tag on screens and reward posts during the event.
  • Keep posting under the tag for a week after the event ends.

Frequently asked questions

Should an event hashtag include the year?

Yes, if the event repeats. The year keeps each edition's posts separate and makes the tag easier to search later.

How do I get attendees to use the event hashtag?

Make it visible everywhere, mention it from the stage, show a live feed of tagged posts, and reward the best posts.

What should I do with event hashtag posts afterwards?

Share a highlights reel with credit, post photos and quotes under the tag, and report the numbers to sponsors.
