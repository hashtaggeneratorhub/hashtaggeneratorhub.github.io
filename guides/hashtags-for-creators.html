Hashtags for Creators: Grow Your Audience Faster

Creators live or die by discovery. Your followers see your posts, but growth comes from strangers finding you through search, tag pages, and the For You feed. Hashtags are one of the few discovery levers you fully control, and a good tag workflow compounds over months.

This guide is a practical workflow for creators: how to pick tags per video, how to build topic lists, how to test, and how to connect hashtag discovery to follows rather than empty views.

Pick tags for the video, not the account

The most common creator mistake is using one fixed tag list for every video. Your account may be about fitness, but a video about stretching is different from a video about meal prep, and the tags should reflect that. Start each video by writing one sentence about what it actually shows, then pick tags from that sentence.

This habit also helps the algorithm. When your tags match the video's content, the platform shows it to the right audience, and that audience watches longer. Longer watch time is what triggers wider distribution, so accurate tags indirectly boost reach through better audience matching.

Build a tag list per content pillar

Most creators have three to five content pillars: the topics they return to again and again. Build a tested tag list for each pillar, with 8 to 12 tags in three sizes. When you make a video in a pillar, start from that list and swap two or three tags for ones specific to the video.

Store the lists in your notes app, with the date you last reviewed them. Review each list every two months: check post counts, drop dead tags, and add new ones you discovered. This takes twenty minutes and keeps your tags fresh without daily effort.

Connect discovery to follows

A view from a hashtag is only valuable if it turns into a follow. End your videos with a clear reason to follow: the next part of a series, a weekly tip, or a free guide. Put the same promise in your bio, so a visitor who taps your profile sees it immediately.

Check which tag sets bring profile visits, not just views. A tag that brings 1,000 views and 50 follows is worth more than a tag that brings 5,000 views and 2 follows. Your analytics show follows per post, so compare posts with similar view counts and see which tag sets converted better.

A weekly testing routine for creators

Each week, pick one variable to test: a new niche tag, a different tag count, or a new content pillar tag list. Post at least five videos with the change and five without, keeping everything else similar. At the end of the week, compare average views and follows from the two groups.

Write the result down in one line: what you tested, what happened, what you will do next. Over a few months, these lines become a personal playbook that no generic guide can match, because it is based on your audience and your content.

Key takeaways

  • Pick tags per video from what the video actually shows, not from a fixed account list.
  • Build one tested tag list per content pillar and review it every two months.
  • Judge tags by follows and profile visits, not views alone.
  • Test one hashtag variable per week and write the result down.

Frequently asked questions

How many hashtags should a creator use?

Five to fifteen on Instagram, three to six on TikTok, and one or two on X. Test within those ranges and keep what your analytics support.

Should creators use trending hashtags?

Only when the trend fits the video. A forced trend tag brings viewers who leave quickly, and low watch time hurts distribution.

How do I turn hashtag views into followers?

Give viewers a reason to follow, such as a series or a weekly tip, and make sure your bio states that promise clearly.
