Twitter / X Hashtags: Best Practices That Still Work

X, formerly Twitter, is the platform where hashtags were born, and it is also the platform where they matter least per post. On X, hashtags are mainly a way to join a conversation, follow an event, or ride a trend. One or two well-chosen tags will do more for you than a dozen.

This guide covers how hashtags work on X today: how many to use, where to put them, how to join trends without looking like spam, and how to use tags for events, chats, and customer support.

How hashtags work on X

On X, a hashtag turns into a clickable link that opens a search for that tag. People use tags to follow live events, breaking news, TV shows, and sports matches. Unlike Instagram, X does not have a tag-following feature that pushes posts into your feed, so tags are a search and conversation tool, not a distribution channel.

Because posts on X are short and fast-moving, hashtags compete with the message itself. A post stuffed with tags is hard to read, and readers scroll past it. The best X posts use a tag as part of a sentence or add one tag at the end, and let the words do the work.

How many hashtags to use on X

One or two hashtags per post is the standard advice, and it holds up. Data from social media studies consistently shows that posts with 1 or 2 tags get more engagement than posts with 3 or more. X itself recommends no more than 2 tags per post.

If you are live-posting an event, one event tag on every post is correct, because the tag is the point: it groups your posts with everyone else's. Outside of events, resist adding tags just because you can. Ask whether each tag adds readers or just adds noise.

Joining trends and conversations

The trending list shows what people in your region are talking about. Before you join a trend, click it and read the top posts. Some trends are jokes, some are serious news, and some are about topics your brand should not touch. A brand that jumps into a serious trend with a sales message can damage its reputation in minutes.

When a trend fits, add your genuine take, not just the tag. People follow the tag to see interesting posts, and a thoughtful reply or a useful fact earns far more engagement than a generic post with a trending tag attached. Reply to other posts in the trend too: conversation is where X rewards you.

Hashtags for events, chats, and support

Events need a short, unique hashtag announced in advance, printed on screens and slides, and used by the organisers themselves. Keep it under 15 characters so it is easy to type. For a recurring chat, use the same tag every week at the same time so regulars can find it.

For customer support, a tag like #YourBrandHelp lets your team find and answer questions quickly. Make sure someone actually monitors it: an unanswered support tag is worse than no tag at all. And for campaigns, pick one tag and use it everywhere, from posts to packaging, so the conversation stays in one place.

Key takeaways

  • Use 1 or 2 hashtags per post on X; more than that reduces engagement.
  • Hashtags on X are for conversations, events, and trends, not for reach farming.
  • Read a trend before joining it, and add a genuine take rather than just the tag.
  • Keep event and campaign tags short, unique, and consistent everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

How many hashtags should I use on X?

One or two per post. X itself recommends a maximum of two, and engagement data supports that limit.

Do hashtags increase reach on X?

Only slightly. Their main value is joining conversations and events. The words in your post and the accounts that engage with it matter far more.

What makes a good event hashtag?

Short, unique, easy to spell, and announced before the event. Check that nobody else is already using it for something different.
